Volume I

SorcMerc

A tactical fantasy RPG on the 2024 fifth-edition rules
In development

Found a mercenary company, hire its blades at roadside inns, and take work from town notice boards. Every fight is turn by turn on a hex board, and every spell you cast stays spent until you can afford a proper night's rest.

What sets it apart

  1. Magic you have to ration

    Spell slots come back only on a long rest, and a long rest costs eight hours of world time, an inn fee or a camp with a chance of ambush. Most 5e games hand slots back after every fight.

  2. Mercs who remember

    Your company earns scars, banes and veteran traits. They form opinions of each other: friends cover each other in a fight, rivals get in the way.

  3. An open world of roads

    March between towns on a living clock. Four countries get harder the farther you go from home, factions go to war, and towns remember how you treated them.

  4. Full 5e rules on hexes

    2024 character creation and the real action economy, played on a hex grid. Surprise, cover and the ground you stand on all count.

  5. Online co-op for up to four

    Share a room code and run one company together. The host leads the march, and everyone fights.

  6. Built to be modded

    Campaigns, monsters and items are plain data packs. The bundled content uses the same tools players get.

Volume II

Horde

A bullet-hell roguelite, set beside SorcMerc's world
In development

Twenty waves, mini-bosses at waves 7, 12 and 16, a boss at 20, and then a final swarm that never stops. You lead a small warband through it, hire and pay companions at camp between waves, and build a squad that can stand in a sea of up to fifteen hundred monsters. Horde is a working title.

What sets it apart

  1. A warband, not a lone hero

    Most survivor-likes give you one character. Here you fight beside companions with their own auras and skills, and you can order them around mid-wave.

  2. Wages, deserters and rivals

    Companions expect pay at every camp. Skip it and they leave, then come back later as rival mercs you have to beat.

  3. Every relic has a catch

    Relics carry a boon and a curse together, and you carry only four. Swapping one out is a real decision.

  4. Couch co-op for up to four

    Two to four players on one screen, each playing a hero. Any empty seat is filled by an AI companion.

  5. Mercs who level up

    Train a class at camp and its basic attack evolves at level three. Named mercs earn quirks and scars, and the fallen go on a memorial.

  6. A weekly challenge

    Each week brings one fixed squad and set of rules for everyone, and any run can be shared by its seed.

The studio
